# Problem statement

## Working problem hypothesis

Many small businesses conduct sales, supplier communication, customer support, and payments through mobile phones, messaging applications, email, and digital-payment services. These workflows can expose owners and employees to social-engineering attacks such as fake payment-detail changes, impersonation, credential requests, suspicious links, and urgent transfer instructions.

Small teams may lack dedicated security staff, formal verification procedures, or affordable tools designed around their daily workflow. A successful scam can therefore have an outsized operational and financial effect.

## What is confirmed at this stage

- The problem area was defined in the March 2026 Aegis Africa competition materials.
- The Phase 0 prototype addresses one narrow part of that idea: helping a user pause and inspect suspicious message and URL indicators.
- The prototype is not evidence that the broader problem has been validated for a particular country, industry, business size, or payment workflow.

## What still requires validation

The following questions must be answered through structured research:

1. Which business types experience the greatest frequency or impact of message-based fraud?
2. Which channels are most relevant: WhatsApp, SMS, email, social media, or another workflow?
3. Who normally receives and verifies payment-detail changes?
4. Which scam categories are common enough to justify a focused product?
5. What language, literacy, accessibility, and trust requirements affect adoption?
6. What do businesses already do when a suspicious request arrives?
7. What price, if any, would be acceptable after value is demonstrated?
8. Which legal, platform, and privacy requirements apply in each target market?

## Claim-control note

The original pitch used statistics about cybercrime losses, African SMB counts, protection levels, attack frequency, and competitor pricing. Those figures are not reproduced here as established facts because their original sources have not yet been verified and documented.
