# Source review and claim audit

## Materials reviewed

The initial repository was prepared after reviewing the following private source materials:

- Written Aegis Africa proposal and pitch script.
- Seven-slide PowerPoint pitch deck.
- HTML pitch presentation.
- Growth Summit 2026 4th-place certificate.
- Growth Summit promotional poster supplied by the founder.

These source files are not copied into the public repository.

## Confirmed and retained

The following points were sufficiently supported by the supplied materials or the founder's direct statement:

- Aegis Africa began as a product concept and solo pitch in March 2026.
- The concept focused on cybersecurity support for African small businesses.
- The pitch proposed WhatsApp-oriented screening, email-fraud monitoring, and awareness training.
- The project received 4th place in the Growth Summit 2026 Pitch Competition.
- The public repository and Phase 0 prototype were organized in August 2026.
- The current prototype is rule-based and contains no AI model.

## Contradictions, overstatements, or unsupported claims

### 1. Present-tense product language

The proposal and decks describe Aegis Africa as if it already scans links, monitors email, works on Android, sets up in under ten minutes, and operates continuously. No supplied source demonstrates an implemented product from March 2026.

**Repository treatment:** Reframed as proposed features. Only the August 2026 rule-based Streamlit prototype is described as implemented.

### 2. Implemented Africa-specific AI

The written proposal and HTML deck state that AI models are trained on African threat patterns. No model, dataset, training code, evaluation, or result was supplied.

**Repository treatment:** The claim is excluded. Machine learning appears only as a future evaluation question.

### 3. Market and cybercrime statistics

The materials use figures including annual cybercrime losses, African SMB counts, protection rates, attack rankings, and the share of attacks attributed to human vulnerability. Citations were not included in the supplied pitch materials.

**Repository treatment:** Figures are treated as historical pitch claims requiring source verification and are not repeated as established facts.

### 4. Competitor and pricing comparisons

The proposal makes broad claims about competitor pricing, English requirements, infrastructure assumptions, percentage cost savings, and the absence of any comparable solution.

**Repository treatment:** These comparisons are excluded pending a dated, sourced competitive review using comparable product tiers and target users.

### 5. Partnerships and distribution

The pitch names OPay, PalmPay, Flutterwave, M-Pesa, banks, fintechs, microfinance institutions, and trade associations as possible channels. No supplied material confirms a partnership, pilot, agreement, or formal discussion.

**Repository treatment:** Companies are not described as partners. Distribution remains a hypothesis for later validation.

### 6. Revenue and impact projections

The proposal calculates potential recurring revenue from hypothetical subscriber counts and describes attacks prevented, businesses protected, and community impact. These are projections, not results.

**Repository treatment:** Revenue, customer, attack-prevention, and impact metrics are excluded.

### 7. Founder education wording

The source materials alternate between "Graduate Diploma" and other educational wording for the Applied AI and Machine Learning program. One script also says the founder spent two years building relevant models, which is not supported by the supplied competition materials.

**Repository treatment:** The repository does not rely on these disputed details. A future founder biography should be checked against official credentials and exact program titles.

### 8. Timing-specific pitch statements

The script says a social-engineering workshop was attended "yesterday." That wording was relevant only to the original presentation context.

**Repository treatment:** Omitted from current project documentation.

### 9. Broad generalizations

Statements such as "every cybersecurity product" being built for Western enterprises, African SMBs having "zero protection," and the target group being unable to recognize phishing are overly broad and unsupported as written.

**Repository treatment:** Replaced with testable problem hypotheses and research questions.

## Information intentionally left blank

The founder mentioned early market contacts, but the repository build did not receive verified details about:

- Who was contacted.
- Whether outreach received a response.
- Whether a substantive conversation occurred.
- When it occurred.
- What was learned.
- What may be published.

The market-discovery file therefore contains a structured template rather than invented entries.

## Rule for future updates

A new claim should be added only when it is one of the following:

1. Directly visible in the repository.
2. Supported by a reproducible test.
3. Supported by a cited, dated source.
4. Supported by a documented research record.
5. Confirmed by an agreement that permits public disclosure.

When evidence is incomplete, label the statement as a hypothesis, proposal, estimate, or planned activity.
